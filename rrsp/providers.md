# RRSP Providers for an Ontario Resident

Researched on April 19, 2026 from official provider and CRA pages.

I focused mainly on `product structure`, `contribution mechanics`, and `transfer process`. Promotional cash bonuses, teaser savings rates, and limited-time transfer offers change often and should be re-checked before opening an account.

## Quick terminology

"RRSP account" is fine shorthand, but the more precise idea is:

- `RRSP` = the registered tax shelter / plan
- `provider` = the bank, brokerage, robo-advisor, or savings institution that offers it
- `product` = the specific RRSP format at that provider, such as:
  - self-directed RRSP
  - managed / robo RRSP
  - RRSP savings account
  - RRSP GIC
  - spousal RRSP

In other words, you usually pick both:

- the `provider`
- the `kind of RRSP`

For Ontario specifically, providers that say you must be the "age of majority in your province" generally mean `18`.

## Important practical note first

If you are moving an existing RRSP from one institution to another, the normal path is a `direct institution-to-institution transfer`, not a withdrawal. CRA says direct transfers generally do **not** use up RRSP room the way a fresh contribution does. If you take cash out yourself and then re-deposit it, that is a different tax event and can trigger withholding tax and room issues.

Also, because today is **April 19, 2026**, the first-60-days contribution window for the **2025** tax year has already ended. CRA's current guide says contributions made from **March 4, 2025 to March 2, 2026** qualified for the 2025 return. A new contribution you make now would normally count toward your **2026** RRSP room instead.

## What to compare between providers

The big differences are usually:

- `product menu`: self-directed brokerage, robo/managed portfolio, cash savings, GICs, spousal RRSP, etc.
- `contribution UX`: linked bank account, Interac, bill payment, payroll/direct deposit, recurring contributions
- `transfer UX`: whether they make inbound transfers easy and whether they support in-kind transfers
- `investments available`: just cash/GICs vs ETFs/stocks/bonds/mutual funds/options
- `fees`: trading commissions, inactivity/admin fees, transfer-out fees, FX costs
- `bank integration`: helpful if you already bank with that institution

## Shortlist by use case

This section is my inference from the sources below, not a provider claim.

- `Wealthsimple`: best if you want a very simple app, easy recurring contributions, and the choice between self-directed and managed RRSPs
- `Questrade`: strong all-rounder if you want both self-directed investing and a robo option, with good transfer/funding paths
- `Qtrade Direct Investing`: strong traditional self-directed brokerage if you want stocks, ETFs, mutual funds, bonds, and GICs in one place
- `RBC Direct Investing`: strongest fit if you already bank with RBC and want tight bank-to-brokerage integration
- `TD Direct Investing / TD Easy Trade`: strongest fit if you already bank with TD and want either a full brokerage or a simpler mobile app
- `EQ Bank`: best if you want a plain cash/GIC RRSP instead of a brokerage RRSP

## Provider Notes

### 1. Wealthsimple

#### What they offer

- Self-directed RRSP
- Managed RRSP
- Registered savings account inside the RRSP umbrella
- Spousal RRSP

Wealthsimple's help centre says you can invest an RRSP through:

- `Self-directed investing`: stocks, options, ETFs
- `Managed investing`: Wealthsimple Portfolios, bond portfolio, money market portfolio, private equity, private credit
- `Savings`: registered savings account

#### Contribution process

- Open online or in the app
- Fund from a Wealthsimple chequing account or other connected funding path
- Recurring transfers are supported from eligible Wealthsimple chequing accounts to RRSPs on weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly schedules
- Interac e-Transfer funding is supported for eligible accounts and funds are available right away
- If you use Wealthsimple direct deposit, you can also "automate your pay" into managed, self-directed, or chequing accounts
- Wealthsimple tracks contributions made within Wealthsimple RRSPs in-app, but explicitly says it does **not** track your total CRA contribution room across other institutions

#### Transfer process

- Wealthsimple supports transfers of RRSPs and spousal RRSPs into both self-directed and managed accounts
- Wealthsimple says RRSP and spousal RRSP transfers typically take about `2-4 weeks`
- Transfers are initiated from the Wealthsimple side in-app/web
- Fractional shares cannot be transferred into self-directed investing accounts

#### Best fit / caveats

- Best fit if you want the cleanest UX and easy automation
- Good if you want one provider that can do both self-directed and managed RRSPs
- Less ideal if you want the broadest possible fixed-income / mutual-fund / bank-branch experience

### 2. Questrade

#### What they offer

- Self-directed RRSP
- Questwealth managed RRSP
- Spousal RRSP

Questrade's RRSP pages say an RRSP there can hold:

- stocks
- ETFs
- options
- mutual funds
- bonds
- precious metals
- GICs
- cash

#### Contribution process

Questrade offers several ways to fund an RRSP:

- `Instant deposit` via Interac or Visa Debit
- `Online bill payment` for CAD
- `Pre-authorized deposit (PAD)` for CAD or USD, including recurring deposits

Notable details from Questrade's learning centre:

- bill payments are their recommended method for Canadian-dollar cash funding and usually arrive in `1-2 business days`
- PADs are their main recurring / USD-friendly option
- PAD setup lets you choose amount, frequency, and start/end date

#### Transfer process

- Questrade lets you transfer almost any investment account over in cash or in kind
- Their transfer-in flow is started from `Move money -> Transfer`
- Typical brokerage transfer timing is `15-20 business days`, though outgoing institutions can make it longer
- Questrade says it covers transfer-in fees up to `$150 per account`
- Like-account rules apply for registered transfers: RRSP to RRSP, TFSA to TFSA, etc.
- Questwealth managed accounts do **not** accept securities in-kind; those need to come over as cash

#### Best fit / caveats

- Strong fit if you want a wide self-directed product menu and also want a robo option available
- Better than some simpler apps if you want GICs, bonds, mutual funds, or in-kind transfers of listed securities
- Slightly more "brokerage-like" and process-heavy than Wealthsimple

### 3. Qtrade Direct Investing

#### What they offer

- Self-directed RRSP
- Spousal RRSP
- Canadian-dollar and U.S.-dollar registered account variants

Qtrade says its registered accounts can hold:

- ETFs
- Canadian and U.S. stocks
- options
- GICs
- bonds
- mutual funds

#### Contribution process

Qtrade funding options include:

- `Direct deposit / EFT`
- `Bill payment`
- `Instant deposit` via Interac

Useful details from Qtrade help pages:

- EFT/direct deposit supports both CAD and USD and can be used for recurring deposits
- bill payments usually arrive in `1-2 business days`
- EFT funds also usually arrive in `1-2 business days`, but Qtrade notes an up-to-`10 business day` hold before those funds can be transferred back out or used for certain trades
- Qtrade does not accept deposits from digital payment processors like Wise, PayPal, KOHO, or Wave

#### Transfer process

- Qtrade supports account transfers from other institutions through its web/app transfer flow
- Transfer types include `all in kind`, `all in cash`, `mixed`, and `partial`
- For other Canadian brokerages, Qtrade says transfers can generally be done electronically
- For some banks / mutual fund companies, a printed and signed transfer form may still be required

#### Best fit / caveats

- Strong fit if you want a classic self-directed brokerage with a wide product menu, including bonds and GICs
- Less "all-in-one modern app" feeling than Wealthsimple
- Watch the admin-fee rules if you expect to keep a smaller balance

#### Fee notes

- Qtrade's pricing page says stock and ETF trades are `$0`
- It lists an account transfer-out fee of `$150`
- It says no administration fee is charged if you meet waiver conditions, including recurring contributions of at least `$100/month`

### 4. RBC Direct Investing

#### What they offer

- Self-directed RRSP
- Spousal RRSP
- Group RRSP
- LIRA / RLSP variants

RBC Direct Investing says its self-directed RRSP can hold:

- stocks
- ETFs
- mutual funds
- GICs
- bonds
- options

#### Contribution process

RBC is strongest if you already bank with RBC:

- cash can be transferred from RBC Royal Bank accounts in `real time`
- this can be done through RBC Online Banking, RBC Mobile, or the RBC Direct Investing site
- RBC also supports a `Pre-Authorized Contribution (PAC)` plan
- PAC frequency can be weekly, bi-weekly, monthly, or quarterly
- PACs can be funded from an RBC bank account online, and RBC says outside-bank PACs can be set up with forms

RBC also notes you can contribute to a registered account with regular or spousal contribution types depending on the RRSP variant.

#### Transfer process

- RBC supports both `cash` and `in-kind` transfers from another institution
- like-account transfer logic applies
- RBC says you should have a statement from the delivering institution showing address, account type, account number, and asset details
- If you bank elsewhere, RBC also says you can add RBC Direct Investing as a bill payee for cash funding from another bank

#### Best fit / caveats

- Best fit if you already use RBC and want easy same-bank transfers into your RRSP
- Good if you want a bank-owned brokerage and the option to use branch / bank support
- Usually less attractive than the low-cost brokerages if your main goal is the cheapest possible self-directed ETF investing

#### Fee notes

- RBC's standard full-suite self-directed pricing is materially higher than Wealthsimple / Questrade / Qtrade
- RBC's public pricing page also shows lighter-cost "GoSmart" RRSP options for new investors, so the exact cost depends on which RBC investing route you use

### 5. TD Direct Investing / TD Easy Trade

#### What they offer

TD is really two RRSP experiences:

- `TD Direct Investing`: fuller self-directed brokerage
- `TD Easy Trade`: simpler mobile investing app

Based on TD's pages:

- TD Easy Trade RRSP is mainly for `stocks and ETFs`
- TD Direct Investing RRSP supports a wider set including:
  - stocks
  - ETFs, including crypto ETFs
  - mutual funds
  - options
  - bonds
  - GICs

#### Contribution process

TD's contribution/funding setup is strongest if you already bank with TD:

- TD bank clients can transfer from EasyWeb or the TD app
- non-TD clients can use Interac e-Transfer from another bank up to their daily limit
- TD also supports recurring deposits through:
  - `Monthly Contribution Plan (MCP)` for registered accounts
  - `Pre-Authorized Deposit (PAD)`
- TD says recurring cash deposits can be set weekly, bi-weekly, semi-monthly, or monthly

TD also notes that depositing at least `$100/month` may help waive quarterly maintenance fees.

#### Transfer process

- TD says you can transfer an account from another brokerage in cash, in kind, or a mix
- TD advertises transfer-fee reimbursement up to `$150` on eligible transfers
- The transfer flow is tied to opening a TD Direct Investing account and then moving assets in

#### Best fit / caveats

- Strong fit if you already bank with TD and want integrated money movement
- TD Easy Trade is the simpler option if you only want a mobile app for basic stock / ETF RRSP investing
- TD Direct Investing is the better fit if you want mutual funds, bonds, GICs, options, or more serious tools
- Pricing is generally higher than low-cost online brokers for normal stock trading

#### Important note

Some TD public RRSP pages appeared inconsistent on contribution-year labeling when I reviewed them on April 19, 2026. I would rely on your CRA account for your actual contribution room and tax-year timing, not TD's headline numbers.

### 6. EQ Bank

#### What they offer

EQ is different from the brokerages above. It is a much narrower RRSP product set:

- RRSP Savings Account
- RRSP GICs

It does **not** offer a stock/ETF brokerage RRSP.

It also says:

- no spousal RRSP
- one EQ Bank RRSP per customer
- multiple RRSP GICs are allowed within the plan

#### Contribution process

- Open in the web interface or mobile app
- Contribute from your EQ Personal Account into the RRSP cash savings account
- EQ explicitly supports recurring automatic contributions
- On cash balances, you earn the posted RRSP savings rate immediately

This is the simplest contribution process of the group if your goal is just "move cash into an RRSP savings product."

#### Transfer process

- EQ supports inbound RRSP transfers from another institution
- You start the transfer from EQ, provide transfer details, and upload a signed transfer form
- EQ says the transfer can take `6-8 weeks`
- EQ says it does **not** reimburse transfer fees charged by your other institution
- EQ also says it does not charge a fee to transfer your EQ RRSP out

#### Best fit / caveats

- Best fit if you want a low-complexity RRSP for cash savings and GICs
- Not a fit if you want ETFs, stocks, mutual funds, or a spousal RRSP
- EQ notes its RRSP is not available in Quebec, but that does not affect you as an Ontario resident

## My take

This section is my inference from the sources above.

- If you want `simple + modern + flexible`, Wealthsimple is the easiest starting point.
- If you want `self-directed with a broad product menu`, Questrade and Qtrade look stronger than the app-first providers.
- If you want `big-bank integration`, RBC and TD become much more attractive if your day-to-day banking is already there.
- If you want `cash/GIC only`, EQ is the cleanest specialized option in this list.

## Questions worth answering before you choose

Before opening anything, I think these are the most useful questions:

- Do you want `self-directed`, `managed`, or just `cash/GIC`?
- Do you need a `spousal RRSP`?
- Are you transferring an existing RRSP, or making a brand-new contribution?
- Do you care more about `lowest fees`, `bank integration`, or `simple UX`?
- Will you contribute manually, or do you want a strong `recurring contribution` setup?
- Do you need to hold `USD`, bonds, mutual funds, or GICs inside the RRSP?
